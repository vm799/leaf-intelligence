Detailed Clinical Evidence Assessment

Atirmociclib Clinical Program

Phase 1/2a (NCT04557449): Established MTD at 600mg with RP2D of 400mg daily. Dose-limiting toxicities included thrombocytopenia and fatigue, consistent with class effects but at lower frequency. Pharmacokinetic profile showed T½ of 24 hours supporting once-daily dosing, reaching steady state by day 15.

Phase 2 TACTIVE-U: Demonstrated ORR of 35% and CBR of 61% in 187 patients including both post-CDK4/6 and first-line settings. Median PFS of 12.1 months compares favorably to historical controls. ESR1 mutations showed similar benefit, suggesting activity independent of resistance mechanisms.

Phase 3 ATTAIN-1: Currently 40% enrolled (205/512 patients) across 150 sites in 20 countries. Open-label design with 1:1 randomization to atirmociclib versus investigator choice CDK4/6 inhibitor. Primary endpoint of PFS non-inferiority followed by superiority testing provides regulatory flexibility. Interim analysis planned at 60% of events (Q4 2025) could support accelerated filing if superiority demonstrated.

Disitamab Vedotin Clinical Program

China Development: Completed trials leading to three NMPA approvals: gastric cancer (2021), bladder cancer (2022), and HER2+ breast cancer with liver metastases (2025). Phase 3 RC48-C006 in 104 patients demonstrated PFS of 9.9 versus 4.9 months (HR 0.56, p=0.0143) establishing efficacy in difficult-to-treat liver metastases population.

Global Expansion: Five Phase 2 trials ongoing globally with 500+ patients enrolled across US, EU, Japan, and China. Combination studies with checkpoint inhibitors show ORR up to 75% in bladder cancer. HER2-low breast cancer activity (ORR 33.3%) expands addressable population beyond HER2+ disease.

Regulatory Considerations: FDA acceptance of China data remains uncertain, likely requiring bridging study or additional Western trial. Pfizer's regulatory expertise and planned US manufacturing may facilitate approval. No ILD signal across 740+ patients provides significant safety differentiation from T-DXd.

177Lu-NeoB Clinical Program

Phase 1 NeoRay (NCT03872778): First-in-human basket trial in GRPR-expressing solid tumors including breast, prostate, GIST, lung, and glioblastoma. Eligibility requires 68Ga-NeoB PET uptake (visual score ≥2) establishing theranostic approach. Dose escalation ongoing to establish MTD and optimal dosing schedule.

Phase 1b Combinations: Three parallel trials (NeoB-002/003/004) each enrolling 48 patients combining 177Lu-NeoB with ribociclib/fulvestrant, capecitabine, or sacituzumab govitecan. Primary endpoints focus on safety and RP2D determination. Current enrollment at 30% suggests 2026 completion for efficacy signals.

Development Challenges: Lack of efficacy data prevents risk assessment. Complex logistics requiring nuclear medicine infrastructure limits addressable sites. Kidney as dose-limiting organ requires careful dosimetry. Long timeline to Phase 3 decision (2027) and potential approval (2033) significantly impacts NPV calculations.

Vepdegestrant Clinical Program

Phase 1/2 Dose Escalation: Established RP2D at 200mg daily with CBR of 51.1% in 147 heavily pretreated patients. Pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic studies confirmed >90% ER degradation at therapeutic doses. Extended treatment duration (median 5.6 months) demonstrated tolerability.

Phase 2 VERITAC: Confirmed activity with CBR 51.5% in 71 patients. Median duration of clinical benefit of 16.5 months exceeded expectations for heavily pretreated population. Biomarker analysis identified ESR1 mutations as predictive of enhanced response.

Phase 3 VERITAC-2: Pivotal trial in 330 patients met primary endpoint with PFS HR 0.57 (95% CI: 0.39-0.85, p=0.0001) in ESR1-mutant subgroup. Overall population showed HR 0.77 (p=0.048), missing statistical significance but showing directional benefit. Superior tolerability with discontinuation rate of 17.2% versus 20.6% for fulvestrant.

Safety & Toxicology Profile Analysis

Comprehensive Safety Assessment

Safety profiles demonstrate clear differentiation enabling competitive positioning. Long-term safety considerations include potential MDS risk (2-3% at 5 years) for 177Lu-NeoB and cumulative peripheral neuropathy for Disitamab.
