Overview

This methodology employs a rigorous, phase-stratified evaluation framework specifically designed for oncology pipeline assessment. The approach emphasizes critical evaluation of trial design, data quality, regional variations, and regulatory alignment, with particular scrutiny applied to uncontrolled early-phase data and region-specific reporting disparities.

Core Principles

  • Evidence Hierarchy: Prioritization of controlled, blinded trials over single-arm studies, with explicit documentation of comparator frameworks
  • Regional Validation: Enhanced scrutiny of Asia-Pacific data with mandatory US/EU corroboration requirements
  • PTRS Integration: Combination of AI-generated probability assessments with subject matter expert validation

Phase 1/1a — First-in-Human Evaluation

PHASE 1 Critical Assessment Points

Key Questions to Answer:

  • Is the study blinded/controlled or purely signal-finding?
  • How were DLTs/MTD/RP2D determined (window, criteria, dose modifications)?
  • How were AEs and PROs elicited (investigator-observed vs patient-reported)?
  • Any placebo anchor available to interpret the treatment effect?
"In these cases, they do a phase one trial... it's not going to be blinded... we just want to see what that treatment effect might be."
— Industry Expert Interview

Data Points Tracked:

  • • Dose levels, DLTs, MTD, RP2D; exposure metrics (RDI, interruptions, reductions)
  • • AE tables (all grades; ≥Grade 3), PRO compliance, data missingness
  • • Any placebo-anchored metrics (even historical) used for context
  • • Site geography and per-region AE/efficacy analyses

Phase 1b — Expansion Cohort Analysis

PHASE 1B Signal Characterization Requirements
Critical Regional Consideration

China-originated Phase 1b data requires enhanced scrutiny due to documented efficacy inflation and AE under-reporting. GSK policy mandates re-running Phase 1 studies in US when licensing Chinese assets.

Validation Requirements:

  • Cohort definitions (lines of therapy, biomarker strata) with sample size justification
  • Durability metrics (DoR, PFS) with adequate follow-up maturity
  • Consistency across regions with US/EU corroboration for China data
  • Biomarker validation with control arm requirement for enrichment designs
"Use of enrichment... problem is the validation of the biomarker... they need to have a control arm of some sort."
— Regulatory Strategy Expert

Phase 2/2b — Signal-Seeking Randomized Studies

PHASE 2 Controlled Trial Requirements

Control Arm & Statistical Design:

  • Control arm justification: Placebo+SoC preferred over investigator's choice due to heterogeneity concerns
  • Sample size & power: Documentation of assumptions and agency alignment
  • PTRS estimation: AI-generated assessments validated by subject matter experts
"The agency tells them what they expect the control arm to be... they basically put a finger in the wind to come up with a sample size."
— Clinical Development Lead

Evidence Synthesis Requirements:

Meta-analysis style evidence roll-up incorporating PubMed literature, ClinicalTrials.gov data, and competitive intelligence. Top 3 comparator programs identified for AI/SME synthesis.

Phase 3 — Pivotal Trial Evaluation

PHASE 3 Registration-Enabling Studies

Critical Assessment Areas:

  • Control arm acceptability: FDA/EMA alignment and "squishiness" in SoC definition
  • Statistical hierarchy: Primary PFS (BICR), key secondaries (OS, PROs), interim analyses
  • Investment magnitude: $100M-1B with appropriate go/no-go thresholds
"In the United States... we'll take a standard of care and add a placebo... approval is usually second line or third line."
— Regulatory Affairs Director

Line of Therapy Considerations:

US approvals typically target 2L/3L settings initially. Assessment must account for evolving SoC and potential label positioning relative to established therapies.

Regional & Bridging Studies

REGIONAL Asia-Pacific Data Validation
Enhanced Scrutiny Required

China-originated data consistently shows efficacy inflation and AE under-reporting due to cultural factors affecting patient-reported outcomes. "Respect factor" leads to systematic under-reporting of adverse events.

Mandatory Verification Steps:

  • Prior work disclosure across ClinicalTrials.gov, IB, and protocols
  • PK/safety comparability assessment to Western populations
  • Identification of US re-runs or bridging studies
  • AE/PRO capture methodology and cultural bias mitigation

Data Quality Framework

Primary Data Sources

  • • ClinicalTrials.gov registry entries and results postings
  • • FDA/EMA regulatory documents and advisory committee materials
  • • Investigator brochures and protocol amendments
  • • Company disclosures and investor presentations
  • • Peer-reviewed publications and conference abstracts

Competitive Intelligence Integration

For breast cancer assets, particularly those originating from China, comprehensive competitive analysis includes evaluation of 2-3 similar programs from major companies. This provides context for efficacy benchmarks, safety profiles, and regulatory strategies.

"If there's two or three breast cancer drugs under development, bigger companies, especially out of China, it's worth looking at all of them from a competitive standpoint."
— Portfolio Strategy Lead

Non-Trial Overlay Requirements

Beyond clinical trial data, comprehensive pipeline evaluation requires systematic assessment of regulatory, commercial, and strategic factors that determine probability of success and investment decisions.

OVERLAY 1 Regulatory Guidance & Interactions

What to Track:

  • Dates & types of meetings (pre-IND, End of Phase 2, SPA, PMDA/NMPA interactions)
  • Regulator guidance on control arms, endpoint hierarchy, safety requirements
  • Label implications ("likely 2L," "combo-only," "accelerated vs full approval")
"The agency tells them what they expect the control arm to be... we have to estimate the probability that this trial is going to hit and what the label is going to look like."
— Regulatory Strategy Expert
OVERLAY 2 Standard of Care (SoC) Dossier

Analysis Requirements:

  • Current global SoC regimens by line of therapy
  • Efficacy benchmarks (ORR, PFS, OS) from landmark RCTs
  • SoC weaknesses and "squishiness" affecting regulatory positioning
"We want to find out everything we can about what that standard of care compound is and where it's been used, where it's been studied... and then write up a report."
— Clinical Development Lead
OVERLAY 3 Probability of Technical & Regulatory Success (PTRS)

PTRS Components:

  • AI-derived PTRS with quantitative scoring
  • SME validation and adjustment rationale
  • Dynamic updates post-interim results or competitor readouts
"We have an AI tool that generates PTRS... frequently wrong, but never in doubt. That's why the subject matter expert has to be there."
— Portfolio Strategy Lead
OVERLAY 4 Competitive Intelligence

Competitive Tracking:

  • All competitor drugs by mechanism, phase, and geography
  • China-origin programs with US/EU expansion plans
  • Funding/licensing deals and partnership structures
Special Focus: China-Origin Programs

Enhanced scrutiny required for Chinese entrants due to data inflation risk. Map all major Chinese programs, especially those seeking Western expansion.

OVERLAY 5 Geographic/Cultural Bias Assessment

Bias Tracking Requirements:

  • Regional distribution (US/EU vs China/Japan/LatAm)
  • AE reporting methods and missingness rates
  • Mitigation strategies (US repeat trials, PRO adaptation)
"Because of the respect factor for the physician... you'll get a bunch of zeros in terms of adverse event reporting... patient reported outcome... inflated."
— Clinical Operations Director
OVERLAY 6 Economic & Financing Analysis

Financial Assessment:

  • Phase 3 estimated cost ($100M–$1B range)
  • Sponsor balance sheet and cash runway analysis
  • Company vs investor progression thresholds
"Do you want to help fund their phase three program, which is... hundreds of millions up to a billion. And the company seemed to have a lower threshold for going forward than we do."
— Investment Committee Member
OVERLAY 7 Operational Disclosure Verification

Documentation Audit:

  • ClinicalTrials.gov completeness and updates
  • Protocol version tracking and amendment analysis
  • Cross-document consistency (CT.gov, protocol, IB)
"In clinicaltrials.gov you will see the prior work... you have to disclose that information in your protocol and your investigator brochure."
— Regulatory Affairs Manager
OVERLAY 8 Line of Therapy & Label Trajectory

Label Positioning Analysis:

  • Trial's intended line of therapy vs likely approval
  • Regulatory precedent for class approvals
  • Commercial trajectory based on label positioning
"In the United States... approval is usually second line or third line, because we still don't know about that standard of care squishiness."
— Market Access Lead

Integration Framework

These nine overlay categories form a comprehensive assessment matrix that supplements clinical trial data. Each asset evaluation must address all categories to provide decision-ready intelligence for investment committees and senior stakeholders.

Critical Success Factor

The non-trial overlay often determines investment decisions more than raw efficacy data. A strong Phase 2 result with poor regulatory alignment or undefined SoC may receive less investment support than a moderate result with clear regulatory pathway and market positioning.
